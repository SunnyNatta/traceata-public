A trip story

Ten years later, we can still walk through our trip.

In April 2016 we crossed the Salar de Uyuni, the world's biggest salt flat, in a Land Cruiser. It was an epic three days. Here's what that trip looks like in Traceata today.

We recorded the drive with a different GPS app, took a few hundred photos with our phones, and then mostly forgot about both. A GPX file in one folder, pictures in another, and a trip that lived in our heads. Years later we imported the track into Traceata, and the whole thing came back together.

The map remembers where

Open the trip and the first thing you see is the route across the salt, with your photos pinned along it. Each little badge is a stack of pictures taken at that spot. Nobody had to tag anything or drag a photo onto a map. Traceata simply looks at when and where each photo was taken, and puts it back where it belongs.

Scroll through the day, chapter by chapter

Under the map, the trip becomes a journal. The day unfolds in chapters, each with its time and how far we'd driven since the last one. The rusty train stuck on its rails. The two of us on the salt with our arms in the air, 38.6 km further on. The little "+28" is the rest of the photos from that stop, one tap away.

Your photos stay in your Photos library. Traceata shows the ones taken during the trip and never copies or uploads them. There's no Traceata server to upload to.

393 photos, 160 km, one very long day

Scroll to the bottom and the numbers are there too: 393 photos and videos, 159.9 km, almost nine hours on the move, somewhere around 3,600 m above sea level the entire time. Run a finger along the speed and elevation charts to see the exact value and the exact time at that point on the road.

Fun fact: nobody remembers the numbers. Everybody remembers the jump. But it's nice to know both are there.

Share it as a photo set, or don't

Sometimes a memory is better when it's shared. Tap Share on a trip, switch the format to Photo Set, and Traceata builds a ready-made series of slides: a cover, the trip's best photos, and a closing map. Each photo slide carries a small route with a dot showing where it was taken.

Pick a collage or the route as the cover, or choose any of your photos. Turn "Plain Photos" on if you'd rather have the pictures without the frames. Then send the slides to a chat, post them as a carousel, or save them to Photos. It all happens on your iPhone. Photo sets, like trip videos and PDF reports, come with the one-time unlock.

You decide what to share, and with whom. Some trips are for the whole group chat, some are for one person, and some are just for you, and that's a perfectly good way to use Traceata. Nothing is shared unless you send it, and we never see your trip at all.

We do this with our own trips. Try it with yours.

Traceata is the app we wanted for ourselves, and it's how we keep our own trips: the route, the photos, a note or two. We love going back through them, and we'd love you to have the same.

You don't even need a new trip to try. Record your next one, or import an old track (GPX and KML from other apps work) and watch the photos from that day find their place on the map.
